# Study 4 — Task-Aware Gearbox Workspace

## Assembly goal

The participant is assembling a four-row educational gearbox on a baseboard.
Each completed row has a gear rod supported between a right and left bearing
stand. Gears are mounted on the rod and retained by wooden pins. The stands are
secured to the baseboard with mounting screws. Row 1 additionally carries the
crank handle used to turn the finished gearbox.

## Parts and assembly sequence

Each row starts with a right gear stand, a left gear stand, two bearings, a gear
rod, one or more gears, wooden retaining pins, and two mounting screws. The
bearings, wooden pins, and screws for a row are supplied together inside that
row's kit. The stands, gear rods, and gears are separate pegboard objects.

The normal assembly sequence for a row is:

1. Insert one bearing into the right gear stand.
2. Put the row's gear or gears onto the gear rod and secure them with the
   appropriate wooden retaining pin or pins.
3. Insert the second bearing into the left gear stand.
4. Use one mounting screw and the compatible screwdriver equipment to fasten
   the right bearing-and-stand assembly to the baseboard.
5. Insert the assembled gear rod through the right stand's bearing, then fit
   the left bearing-and-stand assembly over the other end of the rod.
6. Use the second mounting screw and the same compatible screwdriver equipment
   to fasten the left stand to the baseboard.

The stand, bearing, gear rod, and gears used in later operations may already be
part of a subassembly created by an earlier operation. An existing subassembly
on the gearbox is not a new loose object to retrieve from the pegboard.

## Row-specific parts

### Row 1

Row 1 uses a right gear stand, a left gear stand, two bearings, one gear rod,
one large gear, wooden retaining pins, two mounting screws, and a crank handle.
The large gear is placed on the gear rod and retained with a wooden pin. After
both stands and the gear rod are mounted, the crank handle is attached to the
end of the rod and retained with another wooden pin.

Row 1 mounting screws use the bit screwdriver with the H5 hex bit. The H5 bit
is obtained from Bit Holder 1.

### Row 2

Row 2 uses a right gear stand, a left gear stand, two bearings, one gear rod,
one small gear, one medium gear, wooden retaining pins, and two mounting
screws. The small and medium gears are placed on the gear rod and secured with
their wooden pins before the rod is installed between the stands.

Row 2 mounting screws use the bit screwdriver with the T25 Torx bit. The T25
bit is obtained from Bit Holder 2.

### Row 3

Row 3 uses a right gear stand, a left gear stand, two bearings, one gear rod,
one small gear, one medium gear, wooden retaining pins, and two mounting
screws. The small and medium gears are placed on the gear rod and secured with
their wooden pins before the rod is installed between the stands.

Row 3 mounting screws use the bit screwdriver with the H3 hex bit. The H3 bit
is obtained from Bit Holder 1.

### Row 4

Row 4 uses a right gear stand, a left gear stand, two bearings, one gear rod,
one large gear, wooden retaining pins, and two mounting screws. The large gear
is placed on the gear rod and retained with a wooden pin before the rod is
installed between the stands.

Row 4 mounting screws use the Phillips screwdriver directly; no removable hex
or Torx bit is required.

## Physical workspace and packaging

The pegboard contains independently selectable gear stands, gear rods, gears,
the bit screwdriver, the Phillips screwdriver, and two bit holders. Bearings,
mounting screws, wooden retaining pins, and the Row 1 crank handle are supplied
inside their row's kit. When several named components occupy one kit, that kit
is one selectable physical object.

Reusable fastening equipment is separate from the row kits. The bit
screwdriver is a separate selectable object from Bit Holder 1 and Bit Holder 2.
Selecting an H5, H3, or T25 bit means selecting the holder that physically
contains that bit as well as the compatible bit screwdriver. The Phillips
screwdriver is its own complete selectable tool.

## Row identity cues

The user may identify a row by number, color, or shape:

- Row 1 is the white circle row.
- Row 2 is the red triangle row.
- Row 3 is the green hexagon row.
- Row 4 is the blue square row.

These expressions are equivalent. For example, "Row 2 gear," "red-row gear,"
and "triangle-row gear" all refer to a gear associated with Row 2. A row cue
does not determine left versus right when both sides exist.

Labels ending in `_ASSEMBLY` describe intermediate objects already on the
gearbox. They are useful for reasoning about progress but must not be returned
as loose pegboard acquisition candidates.
